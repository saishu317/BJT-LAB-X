\[ I_C = \min(\beta \times I_B,\; I_{C(sat)}) \]

Flyback Diode Selection (1N4001)

\[ V_{spike} = -L \frac{di}{dt}, \quad V_{clamp} = V_{CC} + V_F \approx 12.7\,\text{V} \]

  Inductive Kickback Protection Analysis

Problem Doorbell buzzers contain electromagnetic coils with high inductance (L). When the BJT rapidly switches OFF (di/dt < 0), the coil generates a dangerous high-voltage inductive spike (V = -L di/dt) that easily exceeds the 2N2222 breakdown voltage (VCEO(max) = 40 V).
Solution Connect a reverse-biased flyback diode (1N4001) in parallel across the buzzer coil.
Rating 1 Peak Reverse Voltage: VRRM ≥ VCC (12 V). 1N4001 provides VRRM = 50 V (> 4× safety factor).
Rating 2 Continuous Forward Current: IF(avg) ≥ ILOAD (100 mA). 1N4001 provides IF(avg) = 1.0 A (10× surge capability).
Result Collector voltage is safely clamped to VC(clamp) = 12 V + 0.7 V = 12.7 V ≪ 40 V (Transistor Fully Protected ✓).
Selected Diode: 1N4001 (50V, 1A General Purpose Rectifier)
  As required by the PS-37 design scope, the 1N4001 flyback diode provides an alternate discharge path for the inductor's stored magnetic energy (\( \frac{1}{2} L I^2 \)) when the transistor switches off, completely preventing transistor punch-through failure.

Why 1N4001 Works in this Circuit

1. Reliable Reverse Blocking: With VRRM = 50 V, it comfortably blocks the 12 V DC bus with negligible leakage current (< 5 µA) while the buzzer operates.
2. High Surge Absorption: When the BJT turns off, the buzzer coil kicks back current (100 mA). The 1.0 A continuous and 30 A surge rating effortlessly circulates the inductive energy without thermal stress.
3. Safe Clamping: Keeps collector voltage locked at VCC + VF ≈ 12.7 V ≪ 40 V (VCEO of 2N2222).

Why 1N4148 Fails in this Circuit

1. Inadequate Current Headroom: The continuous 100 mA buzzer current is at 67% of its absolute max rating (150 mA), leaving zero thermal safety margin.
2. Junction Burnout / Fusion: The 1N4148 has a microscopic silicon die in a glass package with poor heat dissipation. The high dynamic impedance during inductive collapse creates severe I2R heat pulses, leading to thermal runaway and permanent junction burnout.
3. Transistor Vulnerability: Once the 1N4148 fails open, subsequent buzzer turn-off spikes exceed 80 V, instantly destroying the 2N2222 BJT.

Why 1N4733A Fails in this Circuit

1. Breakdown Voltage Below Supply: A flyback diode is reverse-biased across the 12 V supply. Because VZ = 5.1 V < 12 V, the 1N4733A breaks down into continuous reverse conduction immediately when power is applied!
2. Permanent DC Short-Circuit: It creates a continuous low-resistance bypass across the buzzer coil, pulling excessive current from the 12 V rail (> 2 A), starving the buzzer, and overloading the supply.
3. Rapid Thermal Destruction: Dissipating over 5–10 W (well above its 1.0 W rating), the diode overheats to boiling temperature and burns out within seconds.

Engineering Rule of Thumb for Flyback Diodes: A valid flyback diode must fulfill two non-negotiable criteria: (1) Voltage Criterion: \( V_{RRM} > V_{CC} \) (prevents reverse conduction/short-circuit during normal ON states — where 1N4733A fails), and (2) Current Criterion: \( I_{F(avg)} \ge I_{LOAD} \) with high peak pulse rating \( I_{FSM} \) (prevents thermal runaway under high-energy inductive kickbacks — where 1N4148 fails). The 1N4001 satisfies both criteria with generous engineering margins.
